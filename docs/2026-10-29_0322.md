### (b) Uncertainty into Verify AND Abstain — the fuller treatment

What was actually tried (`select_adaptive_gates`): one **global** confidence
probe choosing between two fixed Verify gate schedules; never touched
Abstain. That's why it under-delivered — it wasn't a real test of the idea.

What Propose computes but currently discards: Sinkhorn's transport plan has
a **per-pair** concentration (e.g. 0.95-on-one-candidate vs.
0.4/0.35/0.25-split) — a direct measurement of the "blur" mechanism itself,
at the place it originates. Two genuinely new, additive mechanisms proposed:

1. **Per-correspondence Verify gating** — sharp pairs get a tight gate
   immediately (a wide search radius on an already-correct pair only risks
   Verify locking onto a nearby-but-wrong candidate within that radius —
   the actual blur-to-error mechanism); ambiguous pairs keep the current
   wide schedule so recall on genuinely hard pairs doesn't regress.
2. **Confidence-weighted Abstain threshold** — scale the Wald critical value
   by mean/min Sinkhorn concentration among the fit's inlier
   correspondences, so a fit that got "lucky" on blurred matches is held to
   a stricter bar than one built from unambiguous ones. This is the part of
   the original idea that the gate-width experiment never touched at all.

Both confined to `refine.py`/`abstain.py` call sites, default-off,
Sinkhorn/Kabsch/Wald math itself unchanged. Honest cost: more
hyperparameters (two new mappings to tune on validation) than the first
attempt, so more iteration cycles — but it's the first version that actually
uses Propose's per-pair confidence rather than one global scalar probe.

### (c) LiDAR surrounding-object features — distinct from DINOv2/128-d ROI

Both of Chenyi's already-tried, already-negative directions encode the
object's **own** footprint (RGB crop, or in-box LiDAR points) — low SNR by
construction since that's exactly what's corrupted by the pose error being
corrected. The user's framing is different: **static scene structure around**
a candidate (curbs, poles, building corners) — dense, geometrically
distinctive, seen independently by both agents' LiDAR regardless of whether
either detector boxed it.

Why this targets the diagnosed failure mode specifically (not just "try
appearance again"): the blur happens when several candidates are plausible
under the box-only descriptor; a static context descriptor is a genuine
extra discriminant exactly where that ambiguity exists. This is the opposite
regime from Chenyi's DINOv2 test (sparse 2-agent pairs, already 99.4%
geometric top-1 — "almost nothing for appearance to fix" there). Dense (3+
CAV) scenes are explicitly the untested regime his own limitations section
flags.

Proposed as a **tie-breaker** only for near-tied Sinkhorn mass (reusing (b)'s
concentration signal), using handcrafted pose-invariant descriptors
(height/density rings, FPFH-style surface normals/curvature) rather than a
learned RGB-style encoder — avoids repeating the "entangled with the error"
failure mode.

Honest feasibility: needs a **new raw-point-cloud cache** (current
`alignformer_pva_geometry_train_cache` has boxes/scores only, not raw
points) — real data engineering, not a reuse of existing cache. Should be
sequenced **after** (b) (no new data needed there) and after a cheap
20-50-frame separability check (does the descriptor actually distinguish the
true candidate from the nearby-plausible one in known blur cases?) before
any caching/training investment.

### Recommended priority order

   directly on the diagnosed mechanism.
2. (a) CARLA/AdverCPM smoke test (no training) — richer dense-CAV testbed to
   validate (b) and (c) against than OPV2V's ~10% slice.
3. (c) LiDAR surrounding-context descriptor, validation-only first.
